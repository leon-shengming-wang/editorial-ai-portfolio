# Editorial production & AI evaluation


I’m Leon, a newspaper editor and AI evaluator based in Philadelphia. This portfolio presents the way I approach editorial production and response evaluation. The examples below are illustrative exercises created for this portfolio, not client submissions or published newspaper pages.


## Newspaper production: fitting a story to a page


**The challenge:** A story overflows its assigned space. The editor needs to recover lines while keeping important facts, attribution, and the existing page design intact.


**My workflow**


1. Read the full story and identify the central facts, sourcing, and context.
2. Remove repetition or less essential background before making small cuts to individual lines.
3. Edit in the existing InDesign text frame, preserving typography and paragraph styles. Check the actual reflow after each change.
4. Review the final text for names, numbers, attribution, negation, punctuation, and continuity. Check that the entire story fits within the page.


**Illustrative edit**


- Before: “The city council said on Tuesday that it had approved a new plan to improve safety at intersections across the city.”
- After: “The city council said Tuesday it approved a plan to improve safety at intersections across the city.”


The edit removes redundant words while retaining the decision, attribution, timing, and scope. The available space and surrounding article would determine whether further editing is appropriate.


**Tools:** Adobe InDesign; editorial review and page production.


## AI evaluation: comparing two answers


**The challenge:** A response can sound confident while changing a source’s meaning. Evaluate answers against the prompt and evidence, then explain the decision clearly.


**Illustrative exercise**


- Source: “The committee recommended testing the proposal; it did not approve implementation.”
- Response A: “The committee recommended testing the proposal but did not approve implementation.”
- Response B: “The committee approved the proposal for implementation.”
- **Preference: A.** It preserves both the recommendation and the distinction between testing and approval. B incorrectly states that implementation was approved.


In a full review I would also consider instruction-following, clarity, and other applicable rubric criteria; an unsupported claim can outweigh a more polished presentation.
